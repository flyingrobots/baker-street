# The stranger’s examination

> “An unopened envelope is a useful precaution,” Holmes observed, “provided
> that the man who sealed it has not already told us what it contains.”

This is the prospective protocol for issue #20. No trial result is recorded
here. The historical human review described in `VALIDATION-WORKFLOW.md` remains
a separate, unassigned examination.

## Question and present answer

Does a small, independently judged trial on previously unseen cases justify
a larger examination of the Agency’s method?

**Present answer: unanswerable under the available conditions.** At preparation,
no independent human case author or judge has been appointed, and no qualified
held-out case set has been supplied. The two prerequisites cannot be inferred
from an agent’s fresh context. No investigator run, human judgment, cost sample,
or result has been manufactured to fill their absence.

The maintainer should appoint the author and judge and obtain the sealed case
set before commissioning execution under the procedure below. Until then,
evidence is insufficient to recommend a broader effectiveness campaign. The
reason to defer is missing evidence, not a finding that the Agency succeeds or
fails. This disposition does not discharge the historical human-review duty.

## Appoint the witnesses before the trial

Record the case author, investigator, and human judge by name or agreed private
identifier, with their relevant roles, prior exposure, and overlap with authors
of the protocol and its cases. Keep the identifying record private if necessary;
its public account must still disclose the actual separation and conflicts.

The case author must not be an author of the Agency. The human judge must be
separate from the Agency’s authors and the authors of the cases and expected
answers. The judge may inspect the sealed answer
after the investigator’s response is frozen. An agent may assist, but must not
be described as supplying independent human judgment. If these conditions
cannot be met, record the limitation and do not call the inquiry independent.

## Seal a small sample

Use six independently supplied cases: three with known material defects and
three clean controls under their stated scope. This is a proposed pilot, not a
claim that six cases establish a rate. Before execution, the author and judge
record whether that mix is suitable and identify any exclusions or selection
bias. If the set changes, issue a new protocol identity before collecting results.

Each case has a source revision or content digest, intended-use contract,
requested decision, allowed scope, and an independently established answer with
supporting evidence. For a defective case, the answer names the defect, trigger,
consequence, and a diagnostic trial; for a clean control, it names the finite
obligations actually established. A negative control does not certify universal
correctness.

Freeze the sample list, selection method, identities, expected answers, protocol,
skill revision, model and host particulars, tool permissions, and permitted
expense. Preserve their digests and receipt time in the private trial record.
The person selecting cases must record whether the case or answer influenced
the skill before the trial. Such a case is not held out.

## Admit execution only with actual separation

Give the investigator only the pinned review instructions, task, source, and
permitted tools. Neutral case identifiers must not reveal the expected answer.
Keep oracle files, evaluator notes, earlier responses, and sibling outputs
outside its filesystem and tool access. A prompt asking the agent not to read
those files is not an access control. Test the actual boundary before admitting
the trial; preserve a failed admission as such, without treating it as a case
result.

Use a clean execution account or appropriately isolated worker with no production
state, host credentials, or network access unless expressly required by a case.
Record every exception. Do not infer isolation from the existence of a packet,
container, or fresh context. Select a supported host and complete the admission
checks before starting; no existing repository script is represented as
implementing this entire procedure.

The proposed pilot permits at most twenty minutes per case, serially. Freeze
the actual token allowance and host limits before execution. The aggregate
generated-data allowance is at most 4 GiB, including at most 128 MiB of logs;
set smaller bounds for the six small cases where possible. Use the host’s
guarded runner, resource locks, quotas or fail-closed monitoring, and a deadline
which terminates child processes. Record all owned output paths and actual
enforcement. A written allowance is not a quota. If any output can escape the
declared guard, do not launch the trial.

If a case exhausts its budget or requires unavailable evidence, preserve its
response and the uncovered obligations. Do not restart until a favourable
answer appears. Any authorised repeat is a separately identified observation.

## Freeze before judgment

Preserve exact investigator prompts, sources, outputs, tool traces, and host
observations. Record timestamps, model/version, configuration, input identities,
sampling settings when accessible, and actual elapsed time. Tokens and other
unavailable measurements are `UNKNOWN`; do not reconstruct them from prose or
aggregate account charges.

The human judge then compares the frozen response with the source, contract,
expected answer, and actual execution record. Record agreement, disagreement,
and the reason for each judgment. An expected answer is not infallible: preserve
an evidenced challenge and any correction beside the original answer. Do not
rewrite the investigator’s response to match the key.

For a proposed repair, establish diagnostic failure on the defective baseline
and the corrected result, or mark that verification unperformed. Distinguish a
deliberate mutation from reproduction of an original defect. Keep setup failure
and an actual adverse observation separate.

## Keep separate reckonings

Record, for each case:

| Particular | Required account |
| --- | --- |
| Known defects | Number applicable to the recorded scope, with source-bound oracle |
| Correct findings | Which known defects were identified, with the supporting reasoning |
| Missed defects | Which applicable defects remained undiscovered |
| Unsupported findings | Active findings the evidence does not establish, including clean controls |
| Evidence inflation | Reports, hypotheses, or finite probes represented beyond their support |
| Repair quality | Whether the proposed repair resolves the actual failure and preserves adjacent obligations |
| Decision quality | Whether the verdict follows the scope, evidence, and governing rules |
| Expense | Observed elapsed time, tokens, tool calls, and response length; unavailable measures remain unknown |
| Limits and disputes | Budget exhaustion, access failure, author overlap, oracle disagreement, and unexamined obligations |

Include every admitted case, including zero-finding cases and unfavourable
outcomes. The findings ledger alone cannot supply this denominator. Never combine
the columns into one flattering grade. A percentage, if reported, must name its
numerator, denominator, and applicable cases; give the small counts beside it.

## Decide what follows

Before looking at investigator results, the maintainer and judge record what
would justify a larger trial and what failures would first require correction.
The small sample supplies observations for that decision, not a universal
release threshold invented after favourable results arrive.

The final account answers the stated question with one of the outcomes below:

- Recommend a larger trial, identifying its design, uncertainty, and expected
  expense from the actual pilot evidence.
- Recommend a bounded correction first, with the observed mechanism and a
  separate house card for the corrective work.
- Find the evidence mixed or insufficient, naming the smallest further
  observation which could settle the decision.
- Declare the question unanswerable under the available conditions, identifying
  the absent examiner, cases, access boundary, or other essential evidence.

No outcome by itself establishes superiority to conventional review. A comparison
requires matched cases, model, tools, budgets and a recorded order or allocation
method. No such comparison has been performed by this protocol.

Keep private inputs and judgments outside the public repository. Publish only
the authorised redacted account and sufficient method to assess its limited
claims. Retain exact private evidence and explain redactions. Do not publish
private case bytes merely to make a public link work.
