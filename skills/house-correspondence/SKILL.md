# The Agency’s correspondence

The governing repository instructions prescribe the manner. This guide teaches
its practice; it grants no authority to publish, alter evidence, or decide a case.
Read the relevant editorial provisions before composing a public account.

## Find the distinction before the incident

First establish what the reader must understand or do. Record the actual
constraint, the tempting mistake, and its consequence. Then choose an incident
which makes that distinction visible: amended folios distinguish changed matter
from a fresh reading; a fair copy distinguishes an experiment from an alteration
of the original; a commonplace book distinguishes a remembered impression from
an inspectable account. Do not borrow an incident whose details contradict the
mechanism being explained.

For an extended explanation, Watson supplies the circumstance, a modest human
reaction, or a reasonable objection. Holmes separates the cases and gives his
reason. The conclusion must follow from particulars the reader has seen. Fog,
tobacco, and a familiar name cannot perform that office by themselves.

The README and issues #1–#6 are established models. Consult their relevant
passages when available; access is not required to use this self-contained guide.
All new literary passages are original pastiche, never quotations attributed to
Conan Doyle.

## Fit the letter to its office

A short review reply needs an exact observation, its consequence, and the
proposed correction. It need not invent a visitor to Baker Street. A commit
subject names the alteration in a few words; its body may supply evidence and
limits. A release notice names what the reader receives and what remains
unproved. A substantial explanation may begin with a brief incident and then
give precise technical particulars.

For an issue, begin with the prescribed house card. Preserve its metadata,
execution prompt, headings, and completion conditions. Give narrative chiefly
where it explains background, the problem, urgency, or risk. Do not turn the
acceptance conditions into riddles.

Read [the specimens](references/specimens.md) for contrasting passages and a
complete illustrative issue. Adapt their construction; do not mechanically
repeat their incidents or mistake fictional particulars for evidence.

## Keep the period and the particulars

Write with late-Victorian vocabulary, idiom, syntax, and cadence. Prefer a
concrete object and a reasoned sentence to inflated archaism. Watson observes
and reflects; Holmes is economical and discriminating. Their humour is restrained.
Neither should speak in contemporary commercial slogans or modern conversational
catchphrases merely because the individual words are old.

Necessary commands, paths, identifiers, technical names, and evidence quotations
retain their exact bytes. Set them apart as literals in explanatory passages.
Keep modern technical names outside the invented narration and dialogue. A
quotation of modern evidence remains a quotation of evidence; it does not become
Watson’s speech or license the surrounding prose to change its period.

Literary passages take Markdown blockquotes, except in GitHub issue bodies and
comments. Pull-request correspondence retains blockquotes. Technical tables,
required card headings, and literal records remain exact. Ordinary restrained
explanation needs no quotation merely because its diction belongs to the period.

## Examine the finished account

Read once for meaning: can the intended reader find the outcome, next action,
evidence, and limits without deciphering the story? Compare every command,
identifier, quantity, condition, and negation with its source.

Read again for period: remove contemporary idioms, business language, and modern
rhythms. Ask whether the narrator could have encountered each object and made
each comparison. Replace a strained imitation with a plain period sentence.

Finally inspect the literary quotation marks and blockquotes, the complete
issue-card structure when applicable, and every assurance. Remove ornament
which supplies neither understanding nor character. A structural validator can
check a skill’s form; it cannot certify literary fidelity or factual truth.
